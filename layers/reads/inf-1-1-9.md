The first of the all-layer reads [`../PLAN.md`](../PLAN.md) §4 asks for. A short
passage is displayed at every layer at once and read for what the stack can and
cannot say; each observation is then measured corpus-wide before it is written
down.

**Status.** Nine lines, eight findings, five corpus-wide measurements. Nothing
here is a decision. Findings A–H are observations with counts; §Axes is a
proposal for how to classify them and is the part most likely to be revised by
the next read.

**Update (2026-09-09).** The layer-by-layer review [`../PLAN.md`](../PLAN.md)'s
*Method* since ran against old Layers 2, 4, 5 and `case/` directly (recorded in
[`../REDESIGN.md`](../REDESIGN.md) §§6–9), and several of this read's own
findings and open questions were measured further as part of that work. Each
is noted in place below rather than rewritten, so this file stays what it was:
the first read, not a moving target.

**On gold.** This read opens `skel/`. That is not the harness's prohibition
being broken but the point of `layers/`: under [`../PLAN.md`](../PLAN.md) §2
premise 1, Layer 5 is a *subject* here, not an oracle. What is forbidden in
`harness/` — deriving a rule by matching gold — is not done here either. No rule
is derived at all.

**Why this passage.** It is the corpus's first nine lines, chosen for
no property beyond that. The finding density below is therefore an estimate of

## What works

**Structure that crosses lines is expressed without difficulty.** `mezzo` (1.2)
heads to `ritrovai` (2.2); `selva` (5.2) is the subject of `era` (4.6) on the
line before; `amara` (7.3) coordinates with `dirò` (9.1) two lines later. Layer 3
is per-line and Layer 4 rejoins it, so Layer 5 cites arguments without knowing
enjambment exists. Recording this because the findings below are all costs, and
the line-indexed design is not one of them.

## A. The copula is analysed two ways (1:4 vs 1:7)

The same "A is B" appears in both shapes within nine lines.

| | 1:4 | 1:7 |
|---|---|---|
| Layer 4 | `è` is **root**; `cosa` is **attr** → è | `è` is **cop** → amara |
| Layer 5 | **two** predicates (`è` and `cosa`, both with subject 4.4 `dir`) | **one** predicate (`amara`) |

Corpus-wide: `cop` **988**, `attr` **465**. The head of an `attr` edge is a verb
457 times, and that verb is **`essere` in 357** of them. So *essere* is a
dependent (`cop`) 953 times and a head taking `attr` 357 times.

It is not a nominal/adjectival split: the head of a `cop` is an adjective 710
times but a **noun 191** times, so nominal predicates take both treatments too.

`attr` is a frozen corpus label, documented in
[`../../dep/README.md`](../../dep/README.md) for predicate nominals and secondary
predicates. This is therefore **two conventions, not one convention and one
leak** — which is what makes it a description problem rather than an artifact
defect.

The consequence is heavier than a label disagreement: **the number of Layer-5
predicates depends on which one was picked.** 1:4 yields `(4.3) è` and
`(4.4) cosa`, two tuples asserting the same subject; 1:7 yields one. This is the
generation-side counterpart of [`../PLAN.md`](../PLAN.md) §1.4, where the same
phrase scores 0 or 2 violations by an unmotivated Layer-4 choice.

## B. Nominal roles carried by non-nominal POS — 1,335 positions, three causes

`ch'` at 8.6 and 9.6 has `deprel=obj` but `pos=conjunction`, `lemma=che`. At 6.1
the same relative pronoun is `pos=pronoun`, `case=nominative`. Subject *che* is a
pronoun; object *che* is a conjunction.

Every `che` by role and POS:

```
nsubj  pronoun 1819   relative pronoun 111   conjunction 147
obj    pronoun  355   relative pronoun  28   conjunction  57
obl    pronoun   86   relative pronoun   6   conjunction  21
```

**225 positions** (≈9%) put *che* in a nominal role while tagging it a
conjunction. The POS inventory is also split three ways — `pronoun` vs
`relative pronoun` (153 tokens overall) — which is possible because
[`../../morph/README.md`](../../morph/README.md) states that POS, tense and mood
are collected for later *measure-then-freeze* and are **not enforced**.

**A mistag here silently narrows a layer's coverage.** The case annex is sparse
over pronoun-POS tokens, so a relative pronoun tagged `conjunction` drops out of
the case layer entirely — which is why 6.1 above carries `case=nominative` and
8.6 / 9.6 carry nothing. Layer 2's error is invisible at Layer 2 and shows up as
missing rows two layers away.

**Correction (2026-09-09, `REDESIGN.md` §6): "sparse over pronoun-POS tokens"
overstates it — `case/` is complete and closed over pronoun-POS tokens (100%
coverage, 13,157 rows, an exact match to `case/README.md`'s own count).** The
gate is not a coverage gap inside `case/`; it is `case/case.py:11` reading
Layer 2's `pos` column verbatim to decide which tokens it ever builds a row
for. `ch'` at 8.6/9.6 doesn't lose a case value it should have had — it is
never presented to `case/`'s build step at all, because that step only sees
what Layer 2 already called a pronoun. The observation stands; the mechanism
was one layer further upstream than this read first placed it.

Widening the query to every non-nominal POS in a nominal role gives **1,335**
positions, and they do not have one cause:

| shape | count | examples | cause |
|---|---:|---|---|
| `adverb` → `obl` | 615 | `là` 99, `fuor` 45, `qua` 35, `dove` 33, `dietro` 29, `sù` 25, `retro` 24 | **the language** — locative adverbs really are oblique arguments |
| `verb` → `nsubj`/`obj`/`obl`/`nmod` | 382 | `dir` (4.4), `ch'` heads | **the language** — nominalized infinitives |
| `conjunction` → nominal role | 225 | `che`, `ch'` | **the encoding** — mistag |
| `preposition` / `article` / `interjection` | 11 | | outliers, unexamined |

The 615 are the family [`../PLAN.md`](../PLAN.md) §4-4 leaves open. The 382 are
why Layer 5 sometimes cites a bare token index (`subj 4.4`, `obj 8.6`) instead of
an NP: Layer 3 has no span for an infinitive.

That this split falls out of one query is the encouraging part — see §Axes.

## C. Participle POS is undecided (1:3 vs 1:9)

`era smarrita` tags `smarrita` **adjective**; `ho scorte` tags `scorte`
**verb** (`f. pl. past participle`). POS of every participle carrying an
auxiliary:

```
aux=essere:  verb 468   adjective 213   participle 11   past participle 4   noun 1
aux=avere:   verb 287   adjective  32   participle  5   noun 2
```

Not a rule keyed to the auxiliary: the same lemma takes both under the same
auxiliary — `fare` essere+verb 37 / essere+adjective 2; `giungere` essere+verb 10
/ **essere+adjective 8**; `volgere` essere+verb 13 / essere+adjective 2.

`participle` and `past participle` also appear as POS *values* (22 tokens),
which the unfrozen POS vocabulary (§B) permits.

A, C and H are all "the frozen layer contradicts itself", which is
[`../PLAN.md`](../PLAN.md) §2 premise 2 stated as a measurement rather than a
caution.

**Follow-up (2026-09-09, `REDESIGN.md` §6): the split is three-way, not two,
and it reaches the lemma too.** Widened past `aux`-bearing participles to
every past participle corpus-wide: `pos=verb`+`mood=participle` (724, the
majority), standalone `pos=participle` (25, `tense`/`mood` empty — *Inf* 5:49
`portate`, 10:88/90 `mosso`), and `pos=adjective` (573+, this line's
`smarrita` among them). The third bucket **also splits its own lemma** —
`smarrito` (its own adjectival form) vs. `smarrire` (the verb infinitive) —
for the identical wordform, in 104 distinct wordforms corpus-wide, `smarrito`
itself 4/4. A design response is recorded rather than left as an observation:
stage the classification (coarse POS first, from a closed set, then
per-category refinement) and pin the participle/adjective boundary with an
explicit prompt rule or one-shot example, since staging alone would just
relocate the same three-way split to a coarse/fine boundary (`REDESIGN.md`
§2.1).

## D. `Nel mezzo del cammin` — a complex preposition none of the three devices can hold (1:1)

Layer 4 makes `mezzo` the `obl`; Layer 5 writes
`obl:in [mezzo del cammin di nostra vita]`. But the argument is
**`cammin di nostra vita`**, and `nel mezzo del` is a complex preposition —
"midway along the journey of our life", not "in the middle, which is of the
journey".

This is [`../PLAN.md`](../PLAN.md) §1.3's family (several tokens = one
grammatical word), and **none of §1.3's three devices can express it**:

- Layer 4 `fixed` needs a contiguous run of tokens. `del` is `di+il`: it carries
  **the following NP's article**, so any span containing `nel mezzo del` takes
  that `il` with it. The grammatical word's boundary does not coincide with a
  token boundary.
- A Layer-2 composite POS is one token only.
- A Layer-3 span needs a head, and making `mezzo` the head returns the current
  analysis.

`dietro a` is one of the 168 complex prepositions Layer 4 does hold with `fixed`.
The difference between it and `nel mezzo del` is **whether a contraction
happened** — an orthographic fact, not a grammatical one.

## E. `Tant' … che` — a discontinuous construction, and an ellipsis (1:7)

Two problems in one line.

1. **Correlative.** `Tant'` (7.1, `advmod` → amara) and `che` (7.4, `mark` →
   morte) are the two halves of *tanto…che*. There is nowhere to say they are one
   construction, and `fixed` cannot be that place: it requires contiguity, and
   these are three tokens apart. [`../PLAN.md`](../PLAN.md) §1.3 measured only
   *contiguous* locutions, so this family is entirely unmeasured.
2. **Ellipsis.** `poco è più morte` gaps `amara` ("death is little more so").
   With the predicate elided there is no token to head the clause, so Layer 4
   promotes `morte` to head the `advcl` and makes `poco` its `nsubj`. Layer 5
   then emits

   ```
   (7.2) morte   subj [poco]
   ```

   which is not a possible reading of the line.

Ellipsis is a different family from D and F: the question is whether a layer can
carry an **empty element**, not whether it can carry a hierarchy. The corpus
already has one such device — Layer 5's `subj ∅`, used four times in this passage
— and uses it **for subjects only**. Another device built in one direction.

## F. `mi ritrovai` — orthography decides what is expressible (1:2)

*ritrovarsi* is a pronominal verb; `mi` is part of the lexeme, not an argument.
Layer 4 calls it `expl` and **Layer 5 drops it entirely** (`(2.1) ritrovai
subj ∅`). The Layer-5 predicate slot holds one token, so "the predicate is
*ritrovarsi*" cannot be written.

The same phenomenon **is** expressible when the clitic is enclitic: Layer 2's
composite POS `verb+pronoun` covers **488** tokens (`ritrovòmmi` and its kind) as
one token bearing several categories. Proclitic reflexives — `expl` **1,466**
(`si` 1229, `mi` 125, `ci` 19, `ti` 17, …) — cannot be.

> The same grammatical fact is writable or unwritable according to whether the
> scribe wrote the clitic joined or separate.

**Resolved as far as it can be from the frozen layers (2026-09-09,
`REDESIGN.md` §7): the hypothesis this finding poses survives measurement.**
Every `expl` token's head lemma, paired with the clitic's own lemma, gives 582
distinct verb lemmas under `expl`; **96 of the 170 verb lemmas underlying
`verb+pronoun`'s 488 fused tokens also occur as a separate `expl` construction
with the same clitic** (`andare+si`, `fare+si`, `muovere+si`, …) — the same
verb, the same reflexive, split only by whether the clitic happened to attach
enclitically or stayed a separate word. This is also worse than "unwritable":
`REDESIGN.md` §9 confirms it at Layer 5 too — `derive.py` never references
`"expl"` as a deprel at all, so `mi` in `mi ritrovai` produces no skeleton row
of any kind, not merely no argument slot. The fix in view is not a patch to
old Layer 4: §2.1's rebuilt L2 splits every fused case into two ordinary
entries, at which point `vagliami` and `mi ritrovai` become structurally
identical and there is no longer a reason for whatever derives dependency
relations to treat them differently — the "new device" this finding's own
§Axes entry called for turns out to be L2's split table, one layer down from
where F expected it.

## G. Coordination chains, and nothing checks it (1:5)

`aspra` `conj` → `selvaggia`, `forte` `conj` → `aspra`: a chain, where UD attaches
every later conjunct to the first. Corpus-wide **761 of 4,878 `conj` edges
(15.6%)** have another `conj` as head.

[`../../dep/README.md`](../../dep/README.md) states the convention explicitly,
though only for coordinated objects ("coordinated objects attach the later
conjuncts to the first with `conj`"). Restricting to chains whose first conjunct
is an `obj` — exactly the case the README legislates — the rate is
**60 of 414 (14.5%)**. By first-conjunct role:

```
root 470/2216 (21.2%)   ccomp 85/503 (16.9%)   obj 60/414 (14.5%)
nsubj 44/395 (11.1%)    advcl 41/358 (11.5%)   obl 10/307 (3.3%)
```

Layer 4's `--check` reports **0 soft**: the convention is documented and not
implemented, so nothing measures it.

**Caveat.** Nested coordination produces a legitimate `conj` → `conj`, so 761 is
an upper bound, not a defect count. The weak evidence against a nesting
explanation for most of them is that **606 of the 761** chained pairs share the
same Layer-2 POS with their `conj` parent, which is the flat-list shape rather
than the nested one. This one needs a proper read before any number is claimed.

## H. Layer 3's span granularity is not determined (1:2 vs 1:9)

The same shape — NP plus relative clause — gets 3 spans at line 8 and 7 at line 9,
line 9 peeling one determiner or adjective per level. Corpus-wide, **8,319 of
12,478** child spans share their parent's head (the peeling shape); nesting
reaches depth 6.

[`../../np/README.md`](../../np/README.md) says the layer annotates
"over-inclusively, including nested phrases", so line 9 is the convention and
line 2 — `[una selva oscura]` with no bare `[selva]` child — is the
under-generation. **Not a violation of a stated rule**; what is missing is a rule
saying which projections must be present.

The consequence for consumers: **whether a span exists for a given constituent is
not something a downstream layer can rely on.** If §4-2 evaluates "generalise a
Layer-3-style span layer" as the home for hierarchical judgements, the existing
layer's granularity has to be settled first.

This is the finding held with the least confidence in this file.

**No longer the least confident (2026-09-09): the same defect recurred twice
more, in places this read never looked.** (1) `REDESIGN.md` §2.2, on the
rebuilt NP design: a participle heading a phrase can take an object the way a
verb does, which is internal structure an ordinary determiner/adjective peel
does not need — the same undecided "what hierarchy to build" question line
9's relative clause already posed, not a separate one. (2) `REDESIGN.md` §8,
from `case/`'s own independent read: *Inf* 1:22 `quei` ("*E come **quei** che
con lena affannata, … si volge*") is `nominative` by `case/`'s reading and
attached `obl` by `dep/` to `volse` two lines down — one of 25 corpus-wide
"impossible pairings" (`--stats`), all the same shape: a nominative-form
pronoun heads a relative clause, and the whole pronoun-plus-clause unit fills
an oblique slot up the tree. Neither `case/` nor `dep/` is wrong; a flat tree
has one deprel slot on the pronoun and must pick the outer role, so the inner
one only survives because `case/` was generated blind. Three independent
sightings of the same missing node — this read's own line 9, the participle
case, and the comparative-correlative case — is why `REDESIGN.md` §4.2 now
treats it as one bound to settle, not three.

## Axes

Eight findings in nine lines. That density is itself a result: **the §4-1 survey
cannot be an enumeration** — phenomena do not need hunting. What needs designing
is the classification. The eight sort cleanly into three:

| axis | findings | what closes it |
|---|---|---|
| **(i) the description is doubled** — two conventions for one thing | A (copula), C (participle POS), H (span granularity) | pick a criterion; decidable **inside** the layer |
| **(ii) the check is missing** — a convention exists, unimplemented | G (conj chaining), B's 225 (`che` mistag) | implement it; decidable **inside** the layer |
| **(iii) there is nowhere to write it** — the boundary is locked to tokens | D, E, F, B's 615 and 382 | a new device; a **cross-layer** decision |

If this axis survives the next reads it is a candidate for
[`../PLAN.md`](../PLAN.md) §4-3's "disagreement about the language vs about the
encoding" split — with the refinement that the encoding side divides into (i) and
(ii), which have different remedies and different owners.

**Only (iii) is `layers/`'s own subject.** (i) and (ii) are within reach of the
existing per-layer `CORRECTIONS.md` methodology — classify by cause, verify
against a precedent row, re-run the layer's `--check`.

**Follow-up (2026-09-09): F turned out to be axis (iii) resolved, not axis

### The hypothesis (iii) suggests

[`../PLAN.md`](../PLAN.md) §1.3 diagnoses the corpus as owning three devices with
only one direction built out. D, E and F together support a sharper statement:

> **All three devices are keyed to token boundaries. None is keyed to the
> boundary of a grammatical word.**

`dietro a` is expressible and `nel mezzo del` is not because of a contraction.
`ritrovòmmi` is expressible and `mi ritrovai` is not because of clitic position.
Neither difference is grammatical; both are orthographic. If that holds, §4-2's
"three candidate homes, decided per phenomenon" has the wrong shape, and what is
needed is **one** device — a grammatical-word layer independent of tokenisation.

Stated as a hypothesis to be falsified, not a conclusion. It rests on three
positions in nine lines.

only visible from a consumer's side. Every layer's `--check` is layer-local, and
`dep --check` reports **0 hard, 0 soft** over the full build.

> Every intra-layer check is at zero while 1,335 cross-layer inconsistencies
> stand.

The first tool `layers/` needs is a cross-layer checker. The four queries behind
the numbers above took minutes each; ten more of that kind would classify the
