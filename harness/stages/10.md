## 1. What level 3 has to satisfy before it runs

Unchanged from what level 2 was held to ([`08.md`](08.md) §1) — restated only
because it binds from the design stage, not from the first run.

**Levels are cumulative.** `LEVELS` is read as every class at or below the
requested level (`fixlevel.py:385`), so level 3 *adds* to what levels 1–2 already
close; it does not replace them. A level-3 run therefore re-selects levels 1 and
2 as well, which is also the cheap way to notice that a closed level has
re-opened.

**The three outcomes, from S6.1.** Every candidate class must be resolved to one
of these **before anything is edited**, and the argument must come from the
contract with gold unopened:

1. the artifact is wrong — the only outcome that licenses a fix;
2. the derivation is silent, i.e. a *tolerance* is missing (the mistake Phase 5
   kept making);
3. the two notations are equivalent.

**The soft count is not a quality measure.** Its zero point is
tolerance-mediated — 88 of the registry's 130 rules excuse the 3,250 positions
where gold itself diverges from `derive_unit` — it double-counts relocated
arguments, and it *rises* when a missing predicate is registered. The caveat in
[`../PLAN.md`](../PLAN.md) §2 governs every number this stage will report;
`make agree` stays a readout taken afterwards, never an acceptance criterion
(Standing Invariant §1).

**Check the gate/level alignment first.** S6.10 closed level 1 only after five
corpus-wide runs, by finding that `harness/`'s own transcription of
`validate.py`'s anchor rule was *narrower* than the contract it transcribed — so
the level was asking the session for rows its own gate refused. The property the
harness holds itself to ([`08.md`](08.md) §2, option 3) is exactly this: *whatever a `--fix`
level selects, the session gate admits the row it asks for*. Verify that for
level 3's class before running it, not after four runs.

**The candidates.** The class list and eligibility analysis are in
[`06.md`](06.md) §3 and remain the starting point; what levels 1 and 2 have
already removed from it must be subtracted first. Most of the corpus's 3,126
remaining soft findings have never been inside any level — but "outside a level"
is not the same as "wrong", which is what the three outcomes above are for. For
the scale of what the two existing classes reach, see [`09.md`](09.md) S9.1's
closing paragraph.

**One decision from Stage 6 is still open and is the operator's**: whether
`dante_corpus/skel/repairs.py` is an admissible authority. Stage 8 answered it
**on scope, not on principle** — 297 of its 299 remaining positions lay outside
level 2's selection ([`08.md`](08.md) §5) — so it returns as a live question if
level 3 proposes to select that population.

## 3. Standing rules that apply from day one

Not restated in full — the pointers are the point:

- **Gold is the benchmark, never the target**, and that binds operator-side work
  too (Standing Invariant §1; [`05.md`](05.md) §5).
- **A closed level does not stay closed** (S8.1): read `make fix-level` at
  *every* level after any pass, not just the one that ran.
- **Sweep the per-canto logs before each corpus-wide run** so its telemetry is
  unambiguous, and delete a canto's `.log` and `.tsv` before re-running it under
  a changed implementation (Orientation items 5 and 6).
- **Read bytes and tokens as different quantities** (S9.2), and read a new run's
  `input_tokens` as part of reading the run (S9.3).
- **A stubborn residue is not a proof of unreachability** (S8.5): repeated
  identical refusals bound a per-attempt success rate from above, never at zero.
  Only a mechanism argument establishes that a run cannot reach something — and
  the fix is a mechanism change, never a hand-repair of the leftover positions.

## 4. Milestone Ledger

### S10.1 — Level 3 is `unregistered_predicate`; the scope is fixed (2026-09-06)

The record §1 said would have to come before any run: the candidate audit, the
class argued from the contract with gold unopened, the gate/level alignment
measured *first*, and the mechanism change that argument turned out to require.
No committed artifact was touched and no model was called — this ships the
level, and applying it to the corpus is a separate act needing its own
go-ahead, exactly as S6.2 shipped level 1.

**The residue as it stands, re-read on today's tree** (0 hard / 3,126 soft;
S6.1's structural sub-classification re-run rather than carried over):

| class | count | structure |
|---|---:|---|
| `extra_arg` | 1,878 | 890 L4 children of the predicate under a **non-argument** deprel (714 `advcl`, 115 `advmod`); 511 pro-drop `subj (0,0)`; 464 reached from the predicate by no edge; 13 via the aux/conj head |
| `missing_arg` | 487 | 407 the derivation reached by inference (the propagated subject); 80 the predicate's own `ARG_DEPRELS` child, of which 52 are `xcomp`/`ccomp` |
| `missing_tuple` | 401 | **334 clause heads by their own deprel**; 58 `conj`; 9 elsewhere |
| `role_mismatch` | 184 | 182 label-only disagreements at an L4 argument-child (obj↔subj 59, `obl`→`obj` 18, `xcomp`→`obl:*` 39) |
| `membership` | 133 | **all bare `obl`**; the argument is `advmod` 108 (87 of them adjectives), `mark` 12, `advcl` 7 |
| `extra_tuple` / `dual_role` | 41 / 2 | |

**Three candidates were resolved before one was chosen**, each to one of S6.1's
three outcomes:

1. **`advcl`-as-`obl`, 714 — outcome 2, and therefore not level material.** It
   is the largest single population and the tempting one. `advcl` is not in
   `ARG_DEPRELS`, so `derive.py` says nothing here at all: the derivation is
   silent and the only authority that speaks is registry rule **T**, which
   excuses an `obl:<lemma>` at an `advcl` child carrying a `mark` naming that
   same lemma. 460 of the 571 bare-`obl` cases do carry such a mark (`che` 201,
   `se` 72, `per` 46, `come` 42, `quando` 32, …), so a level that qualified them
   would clear them — *through a gold-fitted tolerance*. That is deriving a
   repair rule from a fit to gold, which Standing Invariant §1 forbids; level 1
   used rule L only **negatively** (the registry declines to excuse that
   direction) with `_oblique_role_of` as the positive authority, and level 3 has
   no equivalent positive authority here. Declined on principle, not on size.
2. **`membership`, 133 — genuinely arguable, and deferred.** Its authority is
   the strongest available: `validate.py`'s own anchor rule, a schema invariant
   rather than a diff against `derive.py`, with all four registry tolerances (AF,
   AQ, DG, DS) already offered the position and declined. But 87 of the 133 cite
   an **adjective** attached as `advmod`, where rule **J** admits an `advmod`
   argument only for an adverb/noun/pronoun and rule **R** names that exact shape
   as `xcomp` — an honest outcome-2 reading that would leave the class about 46
   findings. It stays a candidate for a later level, and the gate defect it
   exposed is recorded as S10.2 below because that defect is real either way.
3. **`missing_tuple`'s clause heads, 334 — outcome 1, and the level.** Chosen.

**Level 3 — `unregistered_predicate`.** The artifact never registers a predicate
Layer 4 makes the head of a clause. Its authority is `derive.py`'s step 1: the
census promotes to a predicate every token whose **own** deprel is in
`CLAUSE_HEAD_DEPRELS`, so the evidence is one tree edge — the token's own — and
the two registry `missing_tuple` tolerances (CS, AV) have already been offered
the position and declined it. The under-complete side is the artifact.

It is the direct continuation of level 2, which named the *argument edge* a
predicate's frame is missing; this names the *predicate* a unit's analysis is
missing. It also answers the question level 2 explicitly left open: level 2
excluded 52 `xcomp`/`ccomp` `missing_arg` findings because a clausal citation is
hard-invalid unless its argument is also a registered predicate, calling that
"a compound repair whose second half is `missing_tuple`'s unargued question".
That question is now argued, so the second half becomes reachable — which is the
main reason this class was preferred over the membership one.

**One restriction, level 2's discipline applied unchanged.** Only a clause head
*by its own deprel* is selected. The census also promotes a conjunct whose `conj`
chain resolves against it (rules CA, AN, BZ) and, in a second pass, any
non-auxiliary verb carrying an argument child; the first is a chain walk rather
than an edge, and the second needs a Layer-2 `pos` a class defined over the tree
cannot see. 67 findings sit on those routes — 58 `conj`, 9 elsewhere — and the
level declines them, as level 2 declines the propagated subject.

**The gate/level alignment, measured before the run and in both directions.**

- *Does the gate admit what the level asks?* Yes, by construction and for once
  trivially: `validate.py` puts no anchor condition on a **predicate** token —
  only that it exist and match its word — so a registration cannot be refused the
  way S6.9's oblique label was. The standing test
  (`test_every_row_any_level_asks_for_is_admissible_to_the_session_gate`) now
  covers a class whose finding names no argument, checking exactly that.
- *Should the level carry a session-side bar?* No, and by a wider margin than
  level 2's. The only bar a gate could carry is "register every clause-head token
  of this unit", which over the committed corpus demands **1,715** positions
  where the level selects **334** — a fivefold over-demand, because the
  derivation's own carve-outs (BN, AN) exclude clause heads from the census and
  the checker excuses two more classes on top. A bar that wrong would refuse the
  model's correct answers, i.e. build S6.9's deadlock deliberately. `toolkit_flags(3)`
  therefore adds none and the ask stays in the notice, as at level 2.

**The mechanism change this level required, and why it is not a weakening.**
`fix_verdict`'s third refusal — no violation *class* the unit did not carry
before — would have refused precisely the correct answers here. Registering a
predicate **exposes its frame**: S6.1 measured 2+ new `missing_arg` at 227 of the
then-490 `missing_tuple` positions, which is why the soft counter is not a
distance and *rises* on a strict improvement. A unit carrying no `missing_arg`
before would therefore be refused with `new_class:missing_arg` for having done
the repair. `FixClass.exempts` is where a class now declares that, narrowly and
positionally: a new divergence finding **at a predicate this level's own findings
named** is arithmetic the repair produced, not a class traded away — that
predicate contributed no findings of those classes before for the plain reason
that it was not on record. Everything else is untouched: the submission must
still be hard-clean, the level's own findings must still strictly fall, and a new
class at any other predicate still refuses. `fixlevel.traded_classes` is the one
implementation, shared by `fix_verdict` and `salvage_by_row`'s per-step copy so
the unit scope and the row scope cannot drift apart. **The standing guarantee is
unchanged — a fix run cannot leave the artifact worse than it found it — but the
soft count is expected to rise on this level, and that is S6.1's point, not a
regression.**

**Scope of a splice.** A level names a row and this level's row does not exist
yet, so `keys` had to be widened to see the tree (as `matches` and `notice`
already do) and enumerates the predicate paired with each of its Layer-4
argument-children, plus its `(0, 0)` slot. A position-scoped splice therefore
takes the new frame exactly where the tree attaches one and nowhere else; an
argument the derivation reaches by inference is level 2's restriction again, and
a splice is not the place to relax it. The whole-unit path is unaffected.

**Selection readout** (`make fix-level FIX=3`, no model call): **334** findings,
all passing `holds`; levels 1 and 2 unchanged at **0**. Suite **1,023 → 1,029**.
`make check` unchanged at 0 hard / 3,126 soft — nothing was applied.

### S10.2 — The session gate does not check anchors for bare `obl` (2026-09-06)

Found while checking S10.1's alignment, filed separately because it is
independent of which class became level 3, and because it is a conformance defect
rather than a design choice.

**The defect.** `harness/runner/tools.py` applies its transcription of the anchor
rule only to `requires_nominal_anchor(role)` — `subj`/`obj`/`iobj`/`obl:<prep>`
(`tools.py:946`), on the reading that "clausal and adverbial roles may anchor on
any token". `validate.py`'s membership check does **not** agree: it exempts only
`''`, `attr`, `xcomp` and `ccomp` (`validate.py:151`), and for a bare `obl` it
grants one further exemption — an argument whose Layer-2 `pos` is an *adverb*
(`validate.py:158`). Bare `obl` is therefore inside the contract's anchor rule
and outside the gate's.

**Consequently the gate has never seen these rows**, and all **133** of the
corpus's `membership` violations are bare `obl`. This is S6.10's finding with the
sign reversed: there the gate was *narrower* than the contract it transcribed,
here it is *wider*. `test_anchor_gate_is_never_stricter_than_validate` binds only
the narrow direction, so the hole passes the suite.

**What closing it would cost, measured rather than guessed.** Over the 3,635
bare-`obl` argument rows the committed corpus holds:

| | rows |
|---|---:|
| the widened gate admits | 3,502 |
| the widened gate refuses and `validate.py` also flags (the 133) | 133 |
| **the widened gate refuses but `validate.py` excuses via AQ / DG / DS** | **0** |

So the three tolerances `tools.py` deliberately left untranscribed — on the
grounds that no position in the corpus needs them, and that transcribing
gold-fitted tolerances into the agent's gate would be the wrong authority — are
still not needed. Closing the hole makes the gate agree with the contract without
importing a single registry rule.

**The one thing to decide first.** At **3** of the 133 positions the argument also
carries a Layer-4 `case` child, which is what level 1's own session bar keys on:
under a level-1 run the level would demand `obl:<lemma>` there while the widened
anchor check refused both `obl` and `obl:<lemma>`, leaving re-anchoring or
deletion as the only gate-clean moves. That is structurally S6.9's deadlock, and
the three positions are `inferno 9` (20,1)/(19,8), `inferno 28` (4,5)/(4,4) and
`paradiso 4` (94,9)/(94,5). **Not fixed here**: the change belongs with whichever
level selects the membership class, and it is a live-run-affecting change to the
session's semantics, so it is the operator's to schedule (Standing Invariant §6).

### S10.3 — Levels 1 and 2 have no reachable residue; the 26 leftovers are outside them (2026-09-06)

Filed because the opposite was assumed in session, and because a wrong assumption
here would have sent a run after positions no run can settle.

`make fix-level` reads **0** at levels 1 and 2, but the classes themselves still
*match* 26 of today's findings — 2 at level 1, 24 at level 2 — all of which the
`holds` precondition declines. The question was whether those are a missed catch
that a widened level ought to pick up. Read position by position, they are not:

- **Level 1's 2** are the shape `_oblique_qualification_holds` documents:
  `rules.py`'s key rewrites (C, BJ) collapse two citations onto one key, so the
  finding reports `'obl'` vs `'obl:<prep>'` at a position whose artifact row
  already *is* qualified (`purgatorio 30` (135,2)/(134,6), `paradiso 6`
  (37,5)/(38,3)). There is no row to repair.
- **Level 2's 24 are not omissions at all.** At every one of them the artifact
  already holds a row at exactly that predicate/argument key — labelled **`subj`**
  — while the derivation reads `obj` (14), `obl` (5) or a qualified oblique (5).
  They surface as `missing_arg` rather than `role_mismatch` because
  `_apply_subj_authority` declares the subject slot LLM-authoritative and accepts
  the artifact's `subj`, leaving the derivation's own role at that position
  unmatched. Adding the derived row would give one position two roles.

So level 2's precondition is declining them **correctly**: this is a
subject/object disagreement in a slot the contract itself hands to the model, not
a frame the artifact left incomplete. The 26 belong with the rest of the 3,126 —
outside every level, with their three-outcome question unargued — and neither
level 1 nor level 2 has anything left to reach. The corollary for planning:
**`make fix-level` at 0 is a complete readout of what a level can still do**, and
a class-level match count is not a to-do list.

### S10.4 — Level 3's first corpus-wide `--fix` run (2026-09-06)

The run S10.1 shipped the mechanism for, executed by the operator across all 100
cantos on three canticle-parallel streams, 08:37 → 15:21 UTC. It is the first
live exercise of both `FixClass.exempts` and — unplanned, and read out here for
that reason — of concurrent execution.

**The numbers, every level read after the pass** (S8.1: a closed level does not
stay closed):

| readout | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| `make check` | 0 hard / 3,126 soft | **0 hard / 2,982 soft** |
| `make fix-level FIX=1` | 0 | **0** |
| `make fix-level FIX=2` | 0 | **0** |
| `make fix-level FIX=3` | 334 | **225** (inferno 88, purgatorio 77, paradiso 60) |
| `make agree` (readout only) | 0.7610 | **0.7628** (inf 0.7672, pur 0.7607, par 0.7605) |
| `uv run pytest -q` | 1,029 | **1,029 passed** |

54 cantos' TSVs changed (+367 / −113 lines). Levels 1 and 2 did not re-open.

**Level 3's own finding count is the criterion and it fell 334 → 225** — the
number that says whether the run worked. 293 units were reopened across 94
cantos: **62 accepted, 20 salvaged, 206 no improvement, 5 refused
`new_class:extra_arg`**.

**The soft count fell, where S10.1 predicted a rise — and the prediction was not
wrong about the mechanism.** Registering a predicate does expose its frame; over
the 293 reopened units soft went 590 → 446, exactly the corpus's −144, so nothing
leaked outside them. What S6.1's arithmetic could not say is which side wins in
aggregate: the `missing_tuple` findings closed, plus the `extra_arg`/`missing_arg`
positions that a registered predicate *resolves* (an argument that had nowhere
admissible to attach now has its head), outweighed the newly exposed frames.
**Read the direction as measured, not as guaranteed**: the standing warning that
soft is not a distance and may rise on a strict improvement is unchanged.

**`FixClass.exempts` fired, and only where it was argued to.** Since an accepted
unit's `fix` record logs its row delta and no soft counts, the check was done
offline: the committed tree at `HEAD` was materialised beside the working tree
and both were re-validated per reopened unit. Three accepted units gained a class
they did not carry before, each at a predicate **this level's own finding named**
— `paradiso 32:73-75` (extra_arg at 74.1, findings 1 → 0), `purgatorio 10:103-105`
(at 105.1, 4 → 0), `purgatorio 23:103-105` (at 104.4, 2 → 0). Without S10.1's
narrowing all three correct answers would have been refused.

**The five refusals are correct, and the exemption is not wider than argued.**
Every one introduces an `extra_arg` at a predicate that is *not* among the unit's
level-3 findings — `inferno 8:52-54` introduces at 53.2 where the findings are
(52,2)/(52,6); likewise `inferno 33:136-138`, `paradiso 4:133-135`,
`purgatorio 6:88-89`, `purgatorio 13:133-135`. The positional key did the work it
was given: same predicate exempt, any other predicate refused.

**No unit ended worse.** Hard stayed 0 everywhere and no unit's level-3 finding
count rose. One unit's soft count rose: `paradiso 7:121-123`, accepted, soft
5 → 6 with findings 2 → 1. It is not a new class — `extra_arg` was already
present, so this passed as plain arithmetic rather than through `exempts` — and it
is the shape S10.1 described from the other side: a `missing_arg` closed, two
`extra_arg` opened, the level's own question answered. The guarantee holds as
stated (hard-clean, the level's findings strictly falling); it was never a
guarantee about the soft *total* of a unit.

**Log hygiene**: all 100 logs carry exactly one `summary` record, so the sweep was
done and the telemetry is one implementation's (Orientation item 5).

**Cost, and the first concurrent run this project has made.** Sum of per-canto
`elapsed_seconds` **16.82 h** against **6.72 h** wall — three streams overlapping
for most of the run (inferno 6.71 h busy, purgatorio 5.21 h, paradiso 4.90 h),
i.e. a measured **2.50× throughput**, against the ≈ 1.7× projection Stage 9
adopted the mode on. 432 requests, **3,564,706 total tokens**, which is
**≈ 8,800 tokens/min against the 16K TPM quota** — about 55% of it at three
streams. **`api_retries` 0, `paced_seconds` 0, `max_length_retries` 0**: no 429
and no contention penalty at this width. This retires the *existence* half of
Stage 9's inherited item 1 — a concurrent run has now been made and it was not
worse than serial — but not its full question: the quota headroom says a fourth
and fifth stream are plausible, and nothing here measures where contention starts.

**The per-request ceiling is still unmeasured** (inherited item 2). The largest
request of the run was **7,007 `input_tokens`**, above every Stage 9 figure
(5,093, 4,085) and still nowhere near a refusal, so the run moved the floor of
what is known and left the ceiling where it was.

**One caveat on recounting.** Re-deriving the pre-run selection offline per unit
gives **335** level-3 findings where `make fix-level` read **334**; after the run
both read 225. The difference is in the recount's unit-by-unit application of
`fixlevel.select`, not in the artifact or the run, and it does not move any
number above — but a later session repeating this reconstruction should expect it
rather than read it as drift.

**The residue is 225 and is not known to be unreachable.** S8.5 stands: repeated
refusals bound a per-attempt success rate from above, never at zero, and 206 of
the 293 units returned no improvement on a single pass. Whether to re-run the
level, and how many passes to budget, is the operator's call — by the units in
the residue, not by the cantos swept (Orientation item 6).
