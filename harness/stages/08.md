## 1. What level 2 has to satisfy before it runs

None of this is new — it is Stage 6's standing method, restated here because
level 2 is the first thing to be held to it from the design stage rather than
mid-flight. The next session starts from these, not from a blank page.

**The mechanism already exists.** Record S6.2 shipped the graded `--fix <level>`
run: it reopens only the units carrying that level's findings and repairs them
**in session**, showing the model the invariant and the frozen-layer evidence
for a position but never the derivation's answer (the scoped reversal of S5.5's
rule). Level 2 therefore needs a *class definition and its selection rule*, not
a new machine — and whatever it defines must be expressible in what
`extractor/fixlevel.py` can select.

**The burden of proof, from S6.1.** The soft counter's zero point is
tolerance-mediated: gold clears the bar only because 88 of the 130 registry
rules excuse the 3,250 positions where gold itself diverges from `derive_unit`,
and those tolerances were fitted by measuring that diff. So the count is a
conformance measure against derivation-plus-registry, not a quality one, and it
is not even a distance — it double-counts relocated arguments and *rises* when a
missing predicate is registered. Every candidate class must therefore be
resolved to one of three outcomes **before anything is edited**:

1. the artifact is wrong — the only outcome that licenses a fix;
2. the derivation is silent, i.e. a *tolerance* is missing (the mistake Phase 5
   kept making);
3. the two notations are equivalent.

The argument must come from `dante_corpus/skel/validate.py` and `derive.py`
**with gold unopened**. Gold-referenced scores (`make agree`) are read
afterwards as a readout, never as the acceptance criterion — Standing Invariant
§1, and the reason the project's own numbers stay meaningful.

**The candidates.** The class list and the eligibility analysis are in
[`stages/06.md`](06.md) §3, unchanged and uninvalidated by the delay. Start
there rather than re-deriving them.

**The precedent to learn from.** Level 1 took five corpus-wide runs (377 → 83 →
37 → 25 → 14 → 12 → 0) and none of S6.5's three session-side levers moved it.
What finally closed it was S6.10: `harness/`'s own transcription of
`validate.py`'s anchor rule was **narrower than the contract it transcribed**,
so the level was asking the session for rows its own gate refused. The lesson
for level 2 is to check that alignment first, not after four runs — which is
exactly what §2 item 1 below is about.

## 4. Milestone Ledger

### S8.1 — Level 2 is `omitted_l4_argument`: the argument Layer 4 hangs on the predicate (2026-09-03)

The stage's design pass and its implementation. **Code and tests only; no
committed artifact was touched and running the level stays the operator's act**
(§3). The level is defined, argued from the contract with gold unopened,
implemented in `extractor/fixlevel.py`, and its gate/selection alignment was
checked *before* any run rather than after four of them — §1's reading of S6.10's
lesson.

**The class.** A `missing_arg` finding is level-2 work when the argument sits
under a Layer-4 edge from the citing predicate whose deprel is in `ARG_DEPRELS`,
and the derived role is nominal. Its authority, read gold-closed: `derive.py`'s
step 2 collects a predicate's arguments as exactly those children, so such a
child *is* an argument of that predicate on the frozen tree; the registry's 20
`missing_arg` tolerances have already been offered the position and declined it
(a finding is what survives them); and the evidence is one tree edge the model
already reads. The artifact is under-complete there — §2's **outcome 1**, the
only one that licenses an edit.

**Why the class is not all 1,639 `missing_arg` findings.** Two restrictions, both
from the contract and not from taste, measured over the committed corpus:

| population | count | why in or out |
|---|---:|---|
| argument-child of the predicate, nominal role | **1,126** | the class |
| subject reached by `conj` propagation (`derive.py` step 3) | 404 | an inference of the derivation, not an edge — no reading of one edge settles it |
| clausal role (`xcomp`/`ccomp`) at an argument-child | 74 | hard-invalid unless the argument is *also* registered as a predicate: a compound repair whose second half is `missing_tuple`'s unargued question |
| stranded on a `cop`/`aux` head (rule AM) | 1 | same reason as the propagated subject |
| under a non-argument deprel | 5 | not an argument edge at all |
| an argument-child the `holds` precondition declines | 29 | the predicate is unregistered, or the artifact already cites that position under a key `rules.py` merged away |

(1,126 + 404 + 74 + 1 + 5 + 29 = 1,639, the corpus's whole `missing_arg` class.)

By derived role the 1,126 are 788 oblique, 211 subject, 113 object, 13 indirect
object, 1 clausal subject; they sit in **770 units across all 100 cantos**, 537
units carrying one and none more than five.

**The alignment check, made first (S6.10's transferable finding).** Two halves:

1. **Can the session write the row the level asks for?** Structurally yes, and
   that is the point: every argument level 2 names sits under an `ARG_DEPRELS`
   edge, which *is* clause AF of `validate.py`'s anchor rule and of
   `tools.anchor_admits`'s transcription of it since S6.10. S6.9's deadlock — a
   level requiring a row its own gate refused — cannot arise here by
   construction, and `test_every_row_level_2_asks_for_is_admissible_to_the_session_gate`
   is what will say so if either side moves.
2. **Should the session carry a bar for this level?** **No, and this is the
   design's one refusal.** A session-side bar sees the frozen layers but not the
   registry, so the only bar it could carry is "cite every Layer-4
   argument-child of every predicate you register" — which demands **2,089**
   positions where the level selects 1,126. That is S6.10's *second* asymmetry (a
   level's bar and its selection naming different positions) at 46% of the pool
   instead of 2 units in 10, so `toolkit_flags(2)` adds nothing and level 2's ask
   lives in the notice, which names exactly the positions the checker selected.

**What crosses into the session.** The same line as level 1: the notice names the
position, the argument's Layer-1 word, and the Layer-4 relation it hangs on —
frozen-layer evidence the model already reads — plus the invariant ("an argument
the tree attaches here belongs in the predicate's frame"). The derived role is
never rendered, and a test asserts its absence in both the notice and the
assembled revision block. The notice also states the reading that declines: if
the model's own analysis makes the token something the predicate does not govern,
the frame stands.

**Mechanism changes, all small.** `FixClass.matches` now takes the canto's Layer-4
rows, because level 2 is the first class defined by the *tree* rather than by the
finding's text — the same `missing_arg` detail is this class at an argument-child
and not at a propagated subject. `dep_rows` therefore threads through
`select` / `class_of` / `governed_keys` / `fix_verdict` / `fix_diagnosis` and into
`check_canto`'s result, since a definition must be applied identically at the
readout, the plan, the acceptance test and the splice. A class that cannot see
the tree selects **nothing** rather than guessing, so a caller that forgets it
under-selects visibly instead of mis-selecting quietly; the end-to-end level-2
CLI test is the guard that the definition reaches `fix_verdict`, where losing it
would silently revert every unit as `no_improvement`.

`holds` (S6.10's precondition) declines a finding whose predicate the artifact
never registered — that is `missing_tuple`'s question — and one whose row the
artifact already carries under a key `rules.py` merged away. `keys` governs the
row the level asks for and, **for a derived subject only**, the predicate's
`(0, 0)` key: a pro-drop `subj (0, 0)` on record and an overt subject the tree
attaches are two fillings of one slot, and without that key a splice would keep
both and leave the predicate with two subjects. No other role touches `(0, 0)` —
the null position is for a dropped subject alone.

**Readouts.** `make fix-level FIX=2` reports **1,128** findings across all 100
cantos (1,126 level-2 + 2 level-1, below); suite **991 → 999**; `make check`
unchanged at **0 hard / 4,624 soft** — this record ships a level, and applying it
to the corpus is a separate act needing its own go-ahead. `FIX` still defaults to
`max`, which now resolves to 2, so an unpinned `make fix` runs both levels; pin
`FIX=1` to hold a run to level 1.

**Cost, so the go-ahead is informed.** 770 units is 2.3× level 1's first pass
(337 units, 61.5 h summed elapsed, 23.9 M tokens), and the ask is larger per
unit. A corpus-wide `--fix 2` should be budgeted at roughly **140 h summed
elapsed / ~65 h wall clock on three `-j3` streams**, extrapolated from S6.3's
per-unit rate — not measured, and the first canticle of a run will price it
better than this estimate does.

**A level-1 regression, found while measuring and left alone.** `make fix-level
FIX=1` reports **2**, not the 0 S6.11 closed at: `inferno 1` `26.2 arg (26,4)`
and `38.4 arg (38,6)`, both bare `obl` where the derivation qualifies. They came
in with the operator's live inferno-1 re-run at `31cb3c7` (the S7.2
confirmation), which is also where the corpus went 4,627 → 4,624 soft — a fresh
session re-answered that canto and re-introduced two findings of a closed level.
Verified pre-existing by running the level-1 selection under the pre-change
module. Nothing was repaired here: a level's findings are cleared by a run, not
by hand (S6.5's rule, and [`../PLAN.md`](../PLAN.md) §4 item 1). It is worth
knowing that **a closed level does not stay closed across later live runs of the
same cantos** — the level table is a standing selection, not a one-time sweep.

**What this record does not claim.** Nothing here has met a model. Level 1's arc
says the count moves on mechanism, not on prompting, and the mechanism this level
most depends on is untested in the field: the splice was built for a *relabel* and
level 2 governs a row the artifact does not have, so `governed_rows.missing` (the
answer declining to write the named row) is the diagnosis field to read first in
the run that follows — S6.9's reading order, set in advance again.

### S8.2 — reserved for the first `--fix 2` run's readout

The operator's run is in flight between sessions; **this record is reserved for

first corpus-wide pass. The baseline it measures against is the S8.1 tree —
0 hard / **4,624 soft**, `make fix-level FIX=2` = **1,128** (1,126 level-2 +
2 level-1 in `inferno 1`), 770 units, suite 999 — re-verified from the pre-run
commit's TSVs rather than taken from this line.

The reading order, fixed here *before* the numbers arrive so the record cannot
be written backwards from them (S6.8's discipline):

1. **`fix.refused.governed_rows`**, ahead of any count. `missing` is level 2's
   own risk: the level names a row the artifact does not have, so an answer that
   declines to write it cannot be spliced — S6.4's mechanism has nothing to take
   at those keys. High `missing` says the level's premise needs arguing at those
   positions; `introduced` off the named rows says the whole-unit ask is what is
   costing the run, as in S6.7.
2. **The refusal mix** (`accepted` / `salvaged` / `no_improvement` /
   `new_class` / `hard`), against S6.11's 10-of-10 accepted. `adopted_invalid`
   does **not** carry S6.3's `--fix` meaning here: level 2 adds no session-side
   bar, so it is an ordinary schema failure.
3. **The counts and the per-class deltas**, with §2's non-distance property in
   hand — writing a `missing_arg` row can clear a paired `extra_arg` at a
   relocated position, so soft may fall by more than the findings did, and that
   is arithmetic rather than corroboration.
4. **Cost against S8.1's estimate** (~140 h summed elapsed / ~65 h wall on three
   `-j3` streams, extrapolated, never measured).
5. **Gold agreement last** (§3), read as a readout and cited as nothing else;
   0.7389 at S6.11 is the standing number.
