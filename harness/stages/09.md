# Stage 9 — Fixed-Context Execution (drafted 2026-09-04, OPENED 2026-09-05)

**Opened by the operator on 2026-09-05**, and opening it is what closes Stage 8
on S8.5 ([`08.md`](08.md) §5) — the convention every stage but 7 has followed.
The document was **drafted a day earlier**, which no earlier stage document was:
§1–§7 below were written on 2026-09-04 while Stage 8 was still running, as a
forecast recorded on the operator's instruction. They are unchanged by the
opening except where §5 records the direction the operator set on 2026-09-05.
Read them as the ground the stage starts from, not as decisions it has made:
the scope below is provisional until a record fixes it, and §8's ledger is where
that happens.

**State at open**, inherited from Stage 8's close: corpus **0 hard / 3,138 soft**,
`make fix-level` **0** at both levels, suite **1,001 passed**, gold agreement
0.7607 (readout only).

**The one thing to settle before building.** §5's open question — an admissible
per-iteration signal $O$ — was the stated reason not to open this stage. It is
still open. Opening the stage does not answer it; it makes answering it the
stage's first work.

**Provisional scope.** Replace the per-unit tool-calling session, on the
reconstruction and repair path, with a **fixed-length execution context**: the
task specification, the unit's frozen-layer evidence, the artifact's current
Layer-5 rows, and a verdict on them — with the model's only output being the
rewritten rows. No tool calls, no growing transcript, and a per-request footprint
that does not increase with the number of iterations.

**Why it is a stage rather than a tidy-up.** §1 is a measurement, not a
preference: a per-request degradation on both backends — retries growing with
context size on the API, prefill slowdown on the local GPU — is what caps a
session at ~3 turns today, and every proposal in Stage 6 through Stage 8 for
iterating a unit toward a better answer has been priced against that
degradation without naming it. What the degradation is *not* is a known
threshold: §1.3's value, in the unit that actually gates a provider, is
unmeasured.

**Origin.** A 2026-09-04 session comparing `harness/` against SKILL.state
(arXiv 2608.26263v3, *Scalable Long-Horizon Agent Skills*). The paper's own
architecture is **not** what is proposed here — §4 says where this design leaves
it deliberately — and the reason to read it is *the paper's* §7 limitations,
which name two of harness's constraints precisely. The operator's reframing is what made it
applicable: the paper is best read not as a memory architecture but as *an
algorithm that keeps context at a fixed length while iteratively improving a
fixed object held inside it*. Under that reading the object is the unit's
Layer-5 rows, and harness already has everything except the loop.

## 1. The finding: context growth, not a turn limit, is what caps the session

All numbers below are from committed code and the disk-only run logs, measured
deterministically in an assistant session (no model was run). **Read §1.4 before
citing any of them**: the logs predate S5.5, and their provenance limits what
they establish.

### 1.1 The tool calls are almost all ceremony

Tool invocations across the four 87-case benchmark logs (348 sessions,
`trace.outcomes`):

| tool | calls | sessions using it |
|---|---|---|
| `read_unit` | exactly **1 per session**, 87 of 87 in each log | 100% |
| `search_corpus` | **4 calls in 348 sessions** | **2.3%** |
| `validate_candidate` | 112 (unit workflow) – 647 (predicate workflow) | ~all |

`read_unit` is a deterministic fetch of the session's *own* unit, with arguments
the runtime already knows; the model spends a turn asking for what could have
been handed to it. `search_corpus` is the only genuinely agentic tool and is
essentially unused. `validate_candidate` is the real work — and it is the
propose/verdict cycle, which is exactly what §2 keeps.

So "remove the tools" is not a bet that the model will cope without them. It is
the observation that **it already does not use them**, apart from one fetch that
is not a decision and one gate that should not be optional.

### 1.3 The degradation, and what is not known about its threshold

**The per-request ceiling is unmeasured** (S9.2, §8). The two
`recon-inf1-*.log` files that carry provider token counts
(`recon-inf1-cap6k.log`, `recon-inf1-verbatim.log`; 210 joined
`llm_request`/`llm_response` pairs) contain **not one request reaching even
13,000 `input_tokens`** — the maximum is 12,999, at a request whose
`context_bytes` was 37,344 (2.87 B/token, the low end of a 2.87–4.01 range,
median 3.56, across both logs). Tokens, not bytes, are the unit a provider
gates on ("16KBではなく16Kトークン。実際に実行してどのくらいを推移するか見ない
と判断不能。"), and no run on disk reaches token volumes where a ceiling would
bind, so establishing where one sits needs a live run, not more reading of
these four logs.

What is established: **both backends degrade a request as it grows**, for
unrelated reasons —

- **Gemini API** (`google:gemma-4-31b-it`): quota → 429 → retry waits.
- **Local** (`ollama:gemma4:31b-it-qat`): a weak GPU, where prefill on a large
  request slows substantially. Operator-reported, **not measured here** —
  every log on disk is a `google:` run.

— and the API side's retry counts rise with `context_bytes` on the same canto
and the same 34 units, monotonically:

| run | median ctx | max ctx | `api_retries` | retry s |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `recon-inf1-compact` | 11,855 | 18,965 | 1 | 15 |
| `recon-inf1-cap6k` | 12,639 | 21,705 | 1 | 15 |
| `recon-inf1-verbatim` | 12,696 | 37,344 | 2 | 79 |
| `recon-inf1-recheck` | 18,555 | 35,172 | 7 | 162 |

Latency, on the API, is **insensitive to context** —
pairing every `llm_request` with its `llm_response` (414 requests) and
normalizing by output size gives 52–57 s per KB of output in every context
bucket from 12 K to 36 K. On this backend larger requests draw more 429s, not
more seconds directly; output size buys seconds.

**The measured session length has no explanation yet.** The mean is **3.3**
turns (348 sessions; 65 of 87 land on exactly 3), and it is not
`SESSION_MAX_TURNS = 12`, which sits far above every observed session. Some
per-request degradation is the natural candidate — the retry table says
degradation is real — but which quantity it degrades past, and at what value,
stays open until a live run measures it in tokens. (An earlier draft closed
this section with $(16{,}000 - 9{,}769) \div 2{,}500 \approx 2.5$ turns beyond
the first, predicting ~3.5; with the 16,000 withdrawn, the agreement with 3.3
is a coincidence of byte-denominated inputs, not a demonstrated cause.)

### 1.4 What these numbers do and do not establish

The four `recon-inf1-*.log` files date from 2026-08-24/26 — the Stage-3/4
boundary — and the benchmark logs from M1.4. They are gitignored and disk-only
([`../PLAN.md`](../PLAN.md) Orientation item 2). They therefore predate S5.5's
artifact-as-resume-state, S7.1's skills split and S7.2's module split.

What survives that: the **shape** — a constant floor dominated by the tool
apparatus,
linear growth per turn, and the ceiling — is architectural and has not changed,
because none of those three records altered the message structure or the tool
set. What does not survive: the **exact bytes**. S7.1 was proven byte-exact
across all eight assembled outputs, so the floor should be unmoved (S9.2, §8,
confirmed this directly against current code), but a stage that opens on this
should re-measure on a current run rather than cite this table. Byte-side rates
in particular are per-canto and inferno 1 is the easiest canto in the corpus;
longer paradiso units will sit higher (S9.2's corpus-wide evidence figures, §8,
bear this out: paradiso's median payload is 3,176 B against inferno's 2,962 B).

## 2. The proposed shape

One bounded step, iterated:

| component | content | budget |
|---|---|---|
| $P$ — specification | the task instruction, no tool specs | 1.0–1.5 KB |
| $O$ — observation | the unit's frozen-layer evidence (L1–L4) and the verdict on the current rows | see §5 |
| $\Sigma$ — the object under repair | the artifact's current Layer-5 rows for the unit | 0.2–0.5 KB |
| output | **the rewritten rows, in full** | — |

and the runtime, not the model, re-gates the output and recomputes the verdict
for the next iteration. Nothing carries across iterations except $\Sigma$.

Three consequences worth stating up front, because each removes a known problem
rather than trading it for a new one:

**The gate stops being optional.** `validate_candidate` is a tool the model
*may* call, which is why `exhausted`, `valid_seen` and `protocol_complete` exist
as failure flags at all. Moving it into the runtime step makes "settled without
ever validating" unrepresentable. S5.5–S5.7 took the corpus to 0 hard precisely
by putting schema checks in front of the model; this puts them in the loop
rather than in a tool the model elects to use.

**Full rows, not a patch.** The paper's state update is a JSON dict merge with
null-deletion semantics, and its own error taxonomy (§5.7) finds that
**68% of Gemma-4-31B's failures are botched merges** — omitted keys during the
update. harness has no reason to inherit this: a unit is a handful of rows, so
the model emits $\Sigma_{t+1}$ directly in the existing TSV column format and
the merge operator is the identity. The paper's dominant failure mode is
designed out rather than mitigated. This is also why the action and the state
update collapse into one output: rewriting the rows *is* the action.

**Reconstruction and repair become the same loop.** $\Sigma_0 = \varnothing$ is
a first reconstruction; $\Sigma_0 = $ the recorded rows is a `--fix`. The
guarantees `extractor/fixrun.py` holds by hand — never leave the artifact worse
than it found it, every refusal path ending on rows already on disk — fall out
of making acceptance the condition for updating $\Sigma$.

### 2.1 The shape stated as types

Not decoration: written this way, three things the prose above argues for
separately turn out to be one property, and a session implementing this mode can
check its work against the signature instead of rediscovering the guarantees.

A step is state-threading over an immutable environment —

```
step :: P -> O -> State Σ ()        --  i.e.  Σ -> ((), Σ)
```

— $P$ in the reader position because it never varies within a run
(Standing Invariant §6 restated), $O$ an input to each step, $\Sigma$ the only
thing that crosses the boundary between steps.

**The value position is empty, and that is the design.** The paper separates
$\Delta\Sigma_t$ from $a_t$ because its actions do something other than update
state (a warehouse `Ship` really ships). Here rewriting the rows *is* the action,
so the value collapses and the step degenerates to an endomorphism
$\Sigma \to \Sigma$. That is the same statement as "full rows, not a patch"
above: dropping the patch is dropping $\oplus$ for the identity, which is why
the paper's dominant failure mode cannot occur.

**Refusal is the identity morphism.** A failing step must leave $\Sigma$ intact,
so the type cannot be `StateT Σ Maybe` — that loses the state along with the
answer. `fixrun.py`'s three exits are exactly the total version: `fix_verdict`
accepts $\Sigma'$, `salvage_outcome` takes a position-scoped partial, and
`revert_outcome` is $\mathrm{id}$. So its standing guarantee —

> a fix run can never leave the artifact worse than it found it, because every
> path out of a refusal ends on rows that were already on disk

— is the statement that **every step is an endomorphism on $\Sigma$ whose
failure case is the identity**, with the three exits choosing from
$\{\Sigma', \Sigma_{\text{partial}}, \Sigma\}$ under a "no worse" ordering.
S6.5 and S6.9's repeat passes, where no canto and no unit ended worse than it
started, are that property measured rather than assumed. A new mode inherits it
by construction or not at all — it is not something to re-derive.

**The model sits in the value position, never the state position.** The oracle
proposes; the deterministic runtime performs every transition. This is the
paper's §7 ("schema ownership resides in the deterministic runtime rather than
the model") and Standing Invariant §4's structure saying the same thing, and it
is precisely what the current design inverts: `validate_candidate` is elective,
so the model holds a piece of the transition, which is why `valid_seen` and
`exhausted` have to exist.

**What the current architecture is, in the same terms.** An append-only
transcript is not `State` but `Writer`: $\Sigma$ never flows, it accumulates as
a log from which the model re-derives the current world every turn. The paper's
whole claim compresses to *stop reconstructing the state from a log and thread
it* — and §1.3's degradation is what that accumulation costs when the log
shares a bounded context with the evidence.

**Where the structure stops, which is the useful part.** `State` says nothing
about a fixed point. Iterating until the verdict is empty (or until
$\Sigma_{t+1} = \Sigma_t$) lives outside the monad, in the `iterate` — and that
is exactly where §5's open question sits. The plumbing is settled; the *metric*
is not, and it is no accident that the one unsolved piece is also the only place
gold-derived information could enter.

One property that falls out and is already relied on: at canto scope the loop is
a fold over the units, `traverse step`, and $\Sigma$ is **serialized** — S5.5's
TSV is the artifact and the resume state at once. "The run resumes" is written up
as an operational convenience; in these terms it is the statement that the state
is serializable, which is what makes the fold restartable across processes. S5.5
did not arrive at that by accident.

## 3. Skills: the one piece that gets *better*

S7.1's file-based skills are not merely compatible with this design — they are
$P$. The paper's own words for $P$ are "the immutable procedural specification,
authored once per domain rather than per task"; `harness/skills.py`'s docstring
calls a skill "the agent's domain knowledge as plain, reviewable files". Under
fixed-context execution $P$ becomes the *only* constant component of the prompt,
so the skill carries more of the design's weight, not less.

**A skill is not just prompt externalization.** It also carries `digest()`, which
is how Standing Invariant §6 is checked after a run
([`07.md`](07.md) S7.1, and `skill_digest` in every `canto_complete`). §1.2 shows
that mechanism currently covers **36.2% of the fixed prompt**: the other 61.3% —
tool specs, XML wire contract, format-demo few-shot — is generated in Python and
is *not* fingerprinted by anything. Removing the apparatus does not merely shrink
the prompt; it brings essentially all of $P$ under the digest, so "which wording
did this run use" becomes fully answerable rather than 36% answerable. **This is
an argument for the design that comes from harness's own invariants rather than
from the paper.**

**What survives verbatim, and what does not.** The current skill is 4,987 B in
four files, and the split is clean along the tool boundary:

- **`SKILL.md` (1,316 B body) — survives.** Row conventions, the frozen role
  vocabulary, `(0, 0)` pro-drop, the NP-head anchoring rule and its clausal
  exceptions: all domain, none of it about tools. Only the phrase "through a
  closed toolset" is tool-bound.
- **`protocol.md` Steps 2 and 4 — survive.** Morphology-driven predicate
  enumeration, agreement and voice, NP heads, control chains.
- **`protocol.md` Steps 1 and 3 — need surgery, not rewriting.** Step 1 opens
  "Call `read_unit`"; Step 3 says "Use `search_corpus` when you need analogous
  constructions" and routes `upstream_feedback` through "your validation call".
  The grammatical content around those clauses is untouched.
- **`step5-unit.md` / `step5-predicate.md` (1,314 B) — absorbed by the
  mechanism.** They are the tool-calling loop, written as instructions because
  the loop lives in the model. In this design the loop is the runtime's, so these
  two files are replaced rather than ported. Note this also erases the
  unit/predicate workflow distinction *within* this mode — the two exist because
  Step 5 can batch or not.
- **The protocol header's anti-hallucination rule gets stronger.** "Never state a
  fact about the text that a tool did not just show you" becomes "…that the
  evidence block does not show you" — and unlike the tool phrasing, that is
  checkable, because everything the model may cite is in the context by
  construction.

**The mechanism for coexistence already exists and needs no change.**
`skills.py` is task-agnostic and `Skill.load()` takes a directory path; only
`runner/prompts.py` binds `GRAMMAR_SKILL` to `skills/grammar-agent`. A second
mode loads a second directory (say `skills/grammar-fixed/`) with its own digest,
and the two modes coexist — Stage 1's benchmark keeps measuring the tool-calling
agent against the skill it was measured on, which is what
[`../TOOLCALL.md`](../TOOLCALL.md)'s ledger and Stage 1's numbers require. S7.1's
byte-exactness was a neutrality proof for *that* refactor, not a permanent
freeze: a new mode is new wording under a new digest, which §6 permits (semantics
change between runs, never mid-run).

**The open risk this creates.** Two skill directories would hold the same domain
knowledge — `SKILL.md`'s row conventions verbatim in both — and duplicated prose
drifts. The frontmatter `resources:` mechanism declares siblings within one
directory and has no cross-directory form, so the options are a shared resource
loaded by both, a test pinning the shared sections equal, or accepting the
duplication deliberately. Worth deciding when the stage opens rather than
discovering after the first divergence.

## 4. Where this leaves the paper

Kept: the bounded footprint, the discarded intermediate reasoning, and schema
ownership sitting in the runtime rather than in the model (the paper's §7, and
Standing Invariant §4's structure already).

Dropped: the JSON state patch (§2 above); the separation of $\Delta\Sigma_t$
from $a_t$; and the claim that the architecture is about memory at all.

**Not applicable, and the reason to be careful:** the paper's §7 limitation (3)
— tasks whose objective *is* the historical trajectory — is Stage 1's entire
purpose. `UnitResult.trace_record()` is the Stage-1→2 contract
([`../PLAN.md`](../PLAN.md) §3), and `extractor/` mines exactly the reasoning
traces the paper discards. So the discard rule may apply only to *what re-enters
the next prompt*, never to what is written to the log — which is S5.5's rule
already (the log is written, never read back). A stage that opens on this must not
weaken the trace record to save bytes.

The paper's §7 limitation (2) — a state update depending on an earlier
observation whose relevance went unrecognized — is the unit boundary, and
harness already commits to it.

Stage 1 and [`../TOOLCALL.md`](../TOOLCALL.md)'s protocol ledger are **not
affected**. `toolcall/` is layer- and task-agnostic, the XML wire decision (T4/T5)
stands, and the benchmark that measures the tool-calling agent keeps measuring
it. What is proposed is a *second execution mode* for the reconstruction and
repair path, not a replacement of the agent.

## 5. The open design question, and the one that is not open

### Open: what may go into $O$

The loop needs a per-iteration signal that the rows got better. The cheap one is
inadmissible.

- **Schema verdict (hard)**: admissible. It derives from
  `dante_corpus/skel/validate.py`, opens no gold, and already runs in-session —
  it is what took the corpus to 0 hard.
- **Soft findings**: **not** admissible as a per-iteration reward. The soft
  counter is mediated by the registry, and 88 of its 130 rules were fitted by
  measuring gold's own divergence from `derive_unit`
  ([`06.md`](06.md) §2). Feeding a recomputed soft count back every turn is
  hill-climbing on a gold-derived proxy — Standing Invariant §4 item 1, reached
  through one indirection. The level-definition discipline (§1 of
  [`08.md`](08.md)) exists precisely to keep that signal out of the session: a
  level is argued from the contract with gold unopened, and only then are its
  positions reopened.
- **Derivation evidence**: admissible, as an *observation* rather than a verdict
  — e.g. which children `derive.py`'s step 2 collects for a predicate, which is
  how S8.1 argued level 2's class in the first place.

**This is the stage's real problem.** The mechanism is straightforward; what is
not settled is an $O$ that makes the iteration converge without becoming a
teaching-to-the-test channel. A stage should not open on this until it has a
candidate answer, because without one the loop has nothing to iterate against.

### Settled by the operator's direction (2026-09-05): $O$ carries everything showable

Stage 8's last finding produced the question in miniature, and the answer to it
generalizes. `purgatorio 27:37` sat unrepaired because the model read the surface
"in su la morte" and wrote `obl:in`, while Layer 4 hangs `su` on `morte` as its
`case` child and `in` as an `advmod` of `su`. Level 1's notice renders that `case`
child; level 2's does not. The operator's ruling — **evidence already shown at one
level may be shown at another** ([`08.md`](08.md) S8.5) — settles that instance,
but the general form is the operator's direction for this stage:

> the fixed context carries **all** the evidence the masking rule permits, and the
> loop runs on top of that.

**What this buys, and it is structural rather than incremental.** Under the
current architecture each `--fix` level decides *per class* which frozen-layer
evidence its notice renders, and every such decision is a fresh argument against
S5.5's line — the `case`-child question is the second one Stage 8 had to make
(S8.1's was the first, about the deprel and the Layer-1 word). A fixed context
that shows everything admissible replaces that recurring argument with a single
boundary drawn once: `derive.py`'s **answer** stays out, the frozen layers go in.
The line does not move — what changes is that it is drawn in one place instead of
re-litigated per class. §1.2's arithmetic says there is room: the tool apparatus
being removed is 6,176 B and the full L1–L4 payload is 2.8 KB, so the evidence
budget grows even as the total shrinks.

**A second reading of Stage 8's residue, which is evidence for the loop itself.**
`purgatorio 27` was answered **eleven times** before it settled, and `inferno 5`
and `purgatorio 7` took eight and ten (S8.4, S8.5). Under the current
architecture each of those is an independent session: the previous answer, the
verdict that refused it, and the reason are all discarded before the next attempt
begins, because a session that kept them would grow into the degradation §1
measures.
So the harness is not iterating on those units — it is **re-rolling** them, and
the observed cost of re-rolling (about eleven samples at ~190 s for one row) is
what a loop with a per-iteration verdict is supposed to replace. This does not
answer the $O$ question above; it prices it.

### Also open, and wider than the draft assumed: the budget

**The budget has a shape but no numbers** (S9.2, §8). The shape holds: some
hard per-request limit, a design target held well below it, the limit
apportioned across $P$/$\Sigma$/$O$. Both numeric inputs an earlier draft used
are gone — the limit itself, which is unmeasured in tokens (§1.3), and a 2.8 KB
evidence figure taken from one canto, where the corpus's median is 3,029 B and
its **maximum 12,199 B**, on evidence alone:

$$|P| + |\Sigma| + |O| \le {?}\ \text{KB (or K tokens), independent of } T$$

with $P$, $\Sigma$ and $O$ apportioned once that bound is known, $\Sigma$ sized
against the corpus's **maximum** (12,199 B for evidence alone, §8 S9.2) rather
than its median, and any plan to pre-render `search_corpus`-style analogues
(§1.1: 2.3% of sessions, so the loss is small and the substitute is cheap)
fitted inside whatever remains of $O$ once $P$ and $\Sigma$'s worst case are
subtracted.

### Not open: that a ceiling exists — open: its value

Both backends impose *some* per-request degradation, for unrelated reasons, so
it cannot be paid off by switching backends (§1.3's retry counts). Open is
everything downstream: the ceiling's **value** in tokens, and therefore what
"well below it" means in the budget above. A live run at token volumes this
corpus's logs never reached is what would settle it. See §7.

## 6. What this re-reads, and what it does not overturn

**S3.7 cut transcript compaction**, on two grounds: it bought 0.5% of the wire,
and it cost the model its own session history. The first ground measured
**total wire bytes**, and §1.3 says the binding quantity is the **per-request
maximum**. In a 3-turn session the growth term is a small fraction of the total
while dominating the tail. The logs appear to contain that A/B directly:
`recon-inf1-compact` (max context 18,965 B) against `recon-inf1-verbatim` (max
37,344 B) on the same canto — compaction roughly halved the tail's peak while
being judged, in S3.7, on the total. That comparison is between two peaks and
does not depend on any threshold drawn across them.

Two cautions on that reading. The pairing is inferred **from the filenames**, not
from a record that names those logs; a session acting on this should confirm it
against [`03.md`](03.md) S3.7 before citing it. And S3.7's *second* ground stands
untouched — compaction did cost the model its history. What §2 proposes is not
the rejected option in cheaper clothing: it does not compress the history, it
makes the history unnecessary by naming the state explicitly, so the cost S3.7
objected to is not incurred. **S3.7 therefore does not foreclose this**, but the
distinction is the whole argument and should be made explicitly in any record
that opens the stage.

Unaffected and not to be re-litigated: S3.10's generation-side cap (output-side,
orthogonal), the S3.9 launch configuration, and every gold-discipline rule in
[`05.md`](05.md) §5.

## 8. Milestone Ledger

### S9.1 — Admissible $O$: registry-free schema plus recomputed frozen-layer observation (2026-09-05)

Answers §5's open question by classifying every `validate_unit` finding
(`dante_corpus/skel/validate.py`) by whether reaching it requires the
soft-divergence registry, then reusing `extractor/fixlevel.py`'s existing line
between a *verdict* and an *observation* rather than inventing a new one.

**The classification.** `validate_unit` (`validate.py:66`) produces two kinds of
finding:

- **Registry-free** — sentinel coexistence, word/position mismatch, duplicate
  rows, self-citing arguments, out-of-unit argument positions, clausal
  (`ccomp`/`xcomp`) citations into a non-predicate, and unknown role vocabulary
  (`validate.py:82`–`126`). None of these calls `rule_active`; they check the
  candidate against the frozen layers and the schema alone. These are exactly
  the hard checks S5.5–S5.7 put in front of the model to take the corpus to 0
  hard, and they are unconditionally **admissible** as $O$: nothing here is
  fitted against gold.
- **Registry-mediated** — the anchor/membership check's four escape hatches
  (rules AF, AQ, DG, DS at `validate.py:160`, `166`, `172`, `175`), the `EG`
  dual-role check (`validate.py:181`, `_dual_role_violations`), and the whole of
  `_classify_divergence` (`rules.py:784`, called at `validate.py:196`) — every
  `missing_tuple`/`extra_tuple`/`missing_arg`/`extra_arg`/`role_mismatch`
  finding, i.e. the entire soft count. `rules.py` calls `rule_active` 78 times;
  `_classify_divergence` is where 88 of the registry's 130 rules earn their
  keep by excusing gold's own divergence from `derive_unit` (S6.1, §2's table
  footnote above). Feeding any of this back as a per-iteration reward is
  Standing Invariant §4 item 1 through the indirection §5 already names.

**What is admissible is not "nothing beyond hard."** `fixlevel.py` already
draws a second line inside the registry-mediated territory, and it is the one
this record adopts: a level's *notice* (`_oblique_qualification_notice`,
`_omitted_l4_argument_notice`, `fixlevel.py:167`, `275`) never states the
registry's verdict — no derived role, no given-vs-derived, no count — only the
**invariant and the frozen-layer evidence that triggers it**, recomputed from
Layers 1–4 directly (`case_children` at `fixlevel.py:66`, `argument_edge` at
`fixlevel.py:87` — both re-derive from `dep_rows` rather than importing
`derive_unit`'s answer). That recomputation is the module's own argument for
why this crosses the masking line safely (module docstring, `fixlevel.py:43`–
`49`): the position and the tree edge are shown, the label is not.

**$O$'s three components, all built from functions that already exist:**

1. **Hard schema verdict** — the registry-free findings above, exactly as
   already runs in-session (S5.5–S5.7). No new code.
2. **Frozen-layer observation, in the `case_children`/`argument_edge` style** —
   for a position the model's own candidate touches, recompute what Layer 4
   attaches there (case children, argument-deprel children, coordination/aux
   heads) straight from `dep_rows`/`morph_rows`, with no `rule_active` call and
   no import from `derive.py`'s output. This is strictly wider than what the
   two fix levels currently render, because it is not filtered to a level's
   `matches()` predicate — the operator's §5 direction ("$O$ carries everything
   showable") says take the full recomputation, not the two classes' slice of
   it.
3. **Fixed point, not a shrinking count.** The loop's stopping rule is the
   verdict going empty or $\Sigma_{t+1} = \Sigma_t$ (§2.1's statement of where
   `State` stops), never a recomputed soft count trending down — that would be
   item (2) above laundered into a scalar reward, which is the one thing §5
   forbids regardless of phrasing.

**What this does not recover.** `fixlevel.LEVELS`' two classes together select
1,503 of the corpus's 3,138 soft findings (377 + 1,126, §2's table). A
registry-free $O$, run to convergence, has no way to ask for a `role_mismatch`
bare-`obl` relabel or a nominal `missing_arg` the way the levels currently
phrase it — those notices exist *because* the registry classified the finding
first. What a registry-free $O$ *can* still surface, because the underlying
tree edges are the same ones `fixlevel.py` reads, is the same frozen-layer
facts a model reasoning from first principles could in any case notice: a
`case` child on its own argument, an unclaimed `ARG_DEPRELS` child of a
registered predicate. Whether the model acting on that alone converges to what
the levels currently force is an empirical question the loop's first live run
answers, not this record — it settles what may be *shown*, not what the model
does with it.

### S9.2 — Re-measurement in tokens, and corpus-wide evidence/$\Sigma$ sizing (2026-09-05)

Discharges §1.4's provenance-limit obligation, and does it under an operator
correction that changed the unit the whole section had to be read in: **"16KB
ではなく16Kトークン。実際に実行してどのくらいを推移するか見ないと判断不能。
多少のオーバーは待てば通ることが多いので、ログだけ取って管理する必要はな
い。"** All three measurements below are deterministic and assistant-side —
read from the disk-only logs and from live code, no model call made.

**1. §1.3's ceiling, reread in tokens.** The two `recon-inf1-*.log` files that
carry provider token counts (`cap6k`, `verbatim`; `compact` and `recheck`
predate that instrumentation and report none) join 210 `llm_request`/
`llm_response` pairs by `(session, messages, attempt)`. Result: **0 of 210
requests exceed 16,000 `input_tokens`**, the maximum being 12,999 at a request
whose `context_bytes` was 37,344 — 2.87 B/token, the low end of a 2.87–4.01
range (median 3.56) that holds across both logs. §1.3's own "16 KB" framing, so
read, is a byte number wearing a token-shaped conclusion; the section's
$2.5$-turn arithmetic and the 3.5-turn prediction it derives are not supported
once the same data is read in the unit that actually gates a provider. This is
now recorded at §1.3 and §7 directly. **What remains unmeasured**: whether a
token ceiling exists at all, and where — no logged request reaches even 13,000
tokens, so a 16K-token boundary (or any other) has never been tested. That is
exactly the operator's second point: a live run is what answers it, not more
reading of these four logs.

**2. §1.2's fixed-prompt floor, reconstructed from current code.** Calling
`runner.prompts.system_prompt`/`few_shot_messages`/`unit_task` and
`runner.tools.tool_specs()` directly (post-S7.1 skill split, post-S7.2 module
split) reproduces **6,176 B, 61.3%** for the tool apparatus against the fixed
prompt's 10,082 B total — byte-exact against §1.2's 2026-08-25 snapshot (6,176
B / 61.3% of 10,088 B). The floor has not moved. (Token counts for this side
are not computed directly — `runner/agent.py`'s `token_usage()` docstring is
explicit that the bytes/token ratio is backend- and content-dependent, "neither
constant nor ours to choose" — so only the measured 2.87–4.01 B/token range
from item 1 is available as an estimate, putting the floor at roughly
2,500–3,500 tokens.)

**3. Evidence ($O$'s raw material) and $\Sigma$, corpus-wide.** §1.2's "2.8 KB"
evidence figure is inferno 1 only (34 units). Calling `GrammarToolkit.read_unit`
(the masked path — no `dante_corpus.skel` gold import anywhere in this
measurement) over every one of the corpus's **3,477 parse units**, at the R1
payload tier, and reading the committed recon TSVs for the matching $\Sigma$
rows:

| | evidence (`read_unit`, JSON) | $\Sigma$ (recon TSV rows) |
|---|---:|---:|
| min | 997 B | 10 B |
| median | 3,029 B | 250 B |
| mean | 3,636 B | 290 B |
| max | 12,199 B (`purgatorio 10:82-93`) | 956 B |

Per canticle the medians are close (inferno 2,962 B, purgatorio 3,014 B,
paradiso 3,176 B) — inferno 1's 2.8 KB was not an outlier by canticle. The
**maximum** is the finding that matters for §5's budget: 12,199 B is **sixteen
times** the 0.5 KB the draft budgets for $\Sigma$ and **twice** the entire 6 KB
it leaves for $O$, on evidence alone, before any observation layer is added on
top. §5's "Also open, but merely quantitative" subsection is now marked
superseded and points here rather than being rewritten — the reasoning shape
holds, the numbers do not.

**What this settles and what it does not.** It settles that the corpus's
worst-case units, not just its median, must be sized before an $O$ budget is
picked — a design that budgets $O$ against the median and meets `purgatorio
10:82-93` will overflow on the first canto that hits it. It does not settle
what the real per-request ceiling is (unmeasured, per item 1) or therefore how
much of that 12,199 B a budgeted $O$ could actually afford to keep — both wait
on the operator's live run, per their own direction, and are not to be
reasoned to from these logs.

**Provenance note.** All three measurements are reproducible from committed
code and disk-only logs/TSVs; no script or output from this record is
committed to the repository (analysis lived in the assistant's scratchpad and
is not part of `harness/`'s durable state, consistent with how S1–S8's
in-session arithmetic has always been reported: as prose in the stage record,
not as checked-in tooling).
